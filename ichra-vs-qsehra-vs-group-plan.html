ICHRA vs QSEHRA vs Group Health Plan: Differences, Costs, and Which Fits

By Jade Benefits | Fort Lauderdale, FL | Updated November 2026

The short answer: a group health plan is one shared plan your employees enroll in. An ICHRA lets you give employees a monthly allowance toward individual plans they choose, at any company size, with no federal dollar cap. A QSEHRA does the same thing for employers under 50 full-time-equivalent employees, but with a yearly IRS cap and no group plan allowed alongside it.

A traditional group plan is not the only way to help your team pay for health insurance. Two reimbursement options, the ICHRA and the QSEHRA, let you give employees a set amount to spend on their own individual plans, and for some small businesses that is a better fit. Here is how the three compare, who each one is built for, and how to decide.

Traditional group health plan

You choose a carrier and plan, and your employees enroll in it. You pay a share of the premium, and the carrier's participation and contribution rules apply. It is simple for employees and familiar, but your costs move with each renewal and your team has a limited set of plan choices. See our participation and contribution rules for what carriers require, and our level-funded plan guide for another way groups can structure the same coverage.

Best for: teams that want one shared plan, can meet the carrier's participation requirement, and value simplicity.

ICHRA (Individual Coverage HRA)

An ICHRA lets an employer of any size reimburse employees tax-free for individual health insurance premiums and, if you allow it, other medical expenses. You set the monthly allowance, and you can vary it by class of employee, such as full-time versus part-time, salaried versus hourly, or by location, within the rules. You can also scale the allowance by age or family size as long as you apply it consistently within a class. Employees pick their own plan on the individual market, which is useful when your team is spread out or wants different doctors and networks.

  • Pros: a fixed, predictable budget; no participation requirement; no federal cap; works for remote or multi-state teams; employees get plan choice.
  • Cons: employees have to shop and enroll on their own; individual-plan premiums and networks can change each year; a high allowance can affect whether employees qualify for marketplace subsidies; there is paperwork to administer.

Best for: businesses that want a fixed budget and flexibility for a varied, remote, or part-time team, or that cannot meet a group plan's participation requirement.

QSEHRA (Qualified Small Employer HRA)

A QSEHRA is built for employers with fewer than 50 full-time-equivalent employees that do not offer a group health plan. You set an allowance up to an annual maximum that the IRS adjusts each year, and employees are reimbursed tax-free for individual coverage and certain medical expenses. For 2026 the maximums are $6,450 for self-only coverage and $13,100 for family coverage. The 2027 amounts had not been published when this guide was updated. Employees must have qualifying health coverage, and you generally have to give them a written notice about the arrangement.

  • Pros: simple and inexpensive to run; fixed budget; no participation requirement.
  • Cons: capped; only for employers under 50 full-time equivalents; cannot be paired with a group plan; the allowance reduces an employee's marketplace subsidy.

Best for: very small employers that want a low-cost, low-admin way to help with premiums.

How each affects ACA subsidies

This is the part most owners miss, and it matters for your employees' take-home cost:

  • Group plan: if the employer plan is considered affordable and meets minimum standards, employees generally cannot also get a marketplace premium tax credit.
  • ICHRA: if the ICHRA is considered affordable for an employee, that employee generally cannot also get a premium tax credit. If it is not affordable, the employee can decline the ICHRA and use the subsidy instead. The IRS sets the affordability percentage each year (9.96% of income for 2026). Employers under 50 full-time equivalents generally do not face the employer penalty, but affordability still decides whether employees keep access to subsidies.
  • QSEHRA: employees can still get a premium tax credit, but it is reduced by the amount of the QSEHRA allowance.

Because of this, the same allowance can help one employee and hurt another, depending on income, age, and location. Our free subsidy calculator shows what an employee may qualify for without the employer plan, and you can read more in our ACA subsidies guide.

Which one fits

  • Choose a group plan if you want one shared plan, your team can meet the participation rules, and you want the simplest setup for employees.
  • Choose an ICHRA if you want a fixed budget with flexibility, have a spread-out or part-time team, cannot meet participation requirements, or want to offer a different benefit to different classes of employees.
  • Choose a QSEHRA if you have fewer than 50 full-time equivalents, no group plan, and want the lowest-cost, lowest-admin option.

The right answer depends on your headcount, budget, and how your employees would react, which is where a broker earns their keep. For the full picture of group options, see our complete small business guide and our guide to choosing a group plan.

How to get started

  1. Count your employees and decide which classes you would offer a benefit to.
  2. Set a budget per person or class, and decide whether to scale it by age or family size.
  3. Price the alternatives. Compare a group plan, an ICHRA, and (if eligible) a QSEHRA for your actual team.
  4. Mind the timing. Individual-market open enrollment for 2027 runs Nov 1, 2026 to Jan 15, 2027, and Dec 15 is the deadline for Jan 1 coverage, so employees choosing individual plans need time to enroll. See the open enrollment dates.
  5. Set up and document the plan, give employees the required notices, and keep records for reimbursements.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an ICHRA and a QSEHRA?

An ICHRA is open to employers of any size, has no federal dollar cap on the allowance, and can be offered by class of employee. A QSEHRA is only for employers with fewer than 50 full-time-equivalent employees, has an annual IRS maximum, and cannot be offered alongside a group health plan.

What is the QSEHRA limit?

The IRS sets the QSEHRA maximum each year. For 2026 it is $6,450 for self-only coverage and $13,100 for family coverage. The 2027 amounts had not been published when this guide was updated, so check the current IRS figure or ask us.

Is there a limit on how much I can give employees through an ICHRA?

There is no federal dollar limit on an ICHRA allowance. You choose the amount, and you can set different amounts for different classes of employees and by age, within the rules. Larger employers also need to think about whether the allowance is affordable under ACA rules.

Can I offer an ICHRA and a group plan together?

Yes, but not to the same class of employees. You can offer a group plan to one class (for example, full-time staff) and an ICHRA to another (for example, part-time staff or employees in a different location). A QSEHRA cannot be offered alongside a group health plan.

Are ICHRA and QSEHRA reimbursements taxable to employees?

When the arrangement meets the rules, reimbursements of qualifying premiums and medical expenses are generally tax-free to employees and deductible to the employer. Confirm the details with your tax professional.

Can my employees still get ACA subsidies if I offer an ICHRA?

It depends on whether the ICHRA is considered affordable. If it is affordable, the employee generally cannot also get a marketplace premium tax credit. If it is not affordable, the employee can decline the ICHRA and use the subsidy instead. A QSEHRA does not block subsidies, but it reduces the subsidy by the amount of the allowance.

Do I need a minimum number of employees to offer an ICHRA?

No. An ICHRA is available to employers of any size, including a business with just a few employees, which can make it an option when a traditional group plan does not fit.

Which is cheaper for a small business, ICHRA or a group plan?

There is no single answer. An ICHRA gives you a fixed budget and no participation requirement. A group plan may cost less per person for a healthy team and gives employees one shared plan. The best way to know is to price both for your team, which is something we can do for free.
