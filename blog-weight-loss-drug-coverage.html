Does Health Insurance Cover Wegovy & Zepbound in 2026?

By Jade Benefits | Fort Lauderdale, FL | Updated 2026

Searches for Wegovy, Zepbound, and GLP-1 coverage have exploded over the past two years, and the honest answer to "will my insurance cover it" is still: it depends, and probably not as fully as you'd hope. Coverage for these medications has actually been narrowing on individual marketplace plans even as demand keeps climbing, while employer group plans are becoming the more interesting battleground, with small business owners now fielding real questions from employees about whether it's covered. Here is where things actually stand in 2026, on both sides.

Why marketplace coverage for GLP-1s keeps shrinking

It seems backwards, demand for these medications has never been higher, yet the share of ACA marketplace carriers covering them for weight loss specifically has been trending down, not up. The reason comes down to cost: GLP-1s are expensive to cover at scale, and insurers pricing individual marketplace plans have been tightening formularies to manage premium increases across the board. The medications are far more likely to be covered when prescribed for type 2 diabetes than for weight loss alone, even though it's frequently the same drug.

If you're shopping individual marketplace coverage and this matters to you, it is worth checking a plan's actual formulary before you enroll rather than assuming coverage exists because a carrier offers the plan type you're used to. Formularies change year to year, sometimes significantly.

What carriers typically require before they approve it

Even plans that do cover GLP-1s for weight loss rarely approve them automatically. Common requirements include:

  • A documented BMI threshold, often 30 or higher, or 27 or higher with a related condition like high blood pressure or type 2 diabetes.
  • Proof of a prior diet and exercise program, typically documented over several months, before the plan will approve the medication.
  • Prior authorization, meaning your doctor's office has to submit paperwork to the carrier before the prescription is approved, not just written.

Worth knowing: even with all the right documentation, some plans still require the medication to be tried and shown ineffective on a cheaper alternative first, a process often called step therapy, before covering it.

The employer decision: what small businesses actually control

This is where it gets directly relevant if you run a small business, and it's also where the most confusion happens. How much say you have over GLP-1 coverage depends on how your plan is funded:

  • Fully-insured plans, what most small businesses have, come with a formulary that's already built by the carrier. You don't get to add or remove specific drug coverage yourself. Your real lever is which plan and carrier you choose at enrollment or renewal, since formularies vary, not customizing the one you're already on.
  • Self-funded and level-funded plans work differently. Because the employer (with a broker and TPA) is designing the benefit itself, it's possible to explicitly include or exclude a GLP-1 or weight-management rider. This is a real option, but it's a different type of plan than standard small group coverage, not an add-on to it.

Either way, employees are increasingly asking about this, and it's becoming a real factor in how competitive a benefits package feels. What we tell small business owners: find out which situation actually applies to your plan before assuming either way, and get the real cost comparison run for your specific group rather than guessing based on a headline number that applies to a much larger employer.

What to do if your plan denies coverage

A denial is not always the final word. A few things worth trying:

  • Ask your doctor's office to appeal with additional documentation. Many initial denials get overturned once a carrier has the full clinical picture.
  • Look into manufacturer savings programs. Some manufacturers offer savings cards that reduce out-of-pocket cost for eligible patients, with or without insurance coverage, though eligibility and pricing change frequently.
  • Confirm the exact prior authorization criteria with your carrier before resubmitting, so you're not guessing at what was missing the first time.

We're not a medical provider and can't advise on treatment decisions, but on the insurance side, we can help you understand what your specific plan requires and whether a different plan at renewal might handle this differently.

Questions worth asking before you enroll or renew

Whether you're shopping individual coverage or reviewing a group plan renewal, these are the questions that actually get you an answer instead of a guess:

  • Does this plan's formulary cover GLP-1 medications for weight loss, not just diabetes?
  • What prior authorization or step therapy requirements apply?
  • Is our plan fully-insured, self-funded, or level-funded, and does that change what we're able to choose?
  • What would GLP-1 coverage change about our group's premium, in real dollars for our headcount?

GLP-1 coverage FAQ

Does health insurance cover Wegovy or Zepbound for weight loss?

It depends on the plan. Coverage for GLP-1 medications prescribed specifically for weight loss has been shrinking on ACA marketplace plans, and most carriers that do cover it require documentation like a BMI threshold, a related health condition, and proof of a prior diet and exercise program before approving it. Coverage is generally more common when the same medication is prescribed for type 2 diabetes rather than weight loss.

Can my small business add GLP-1 coverage to our group health plan?

It depends on how your plan is funded. Most small businesses have fully-insured plans, where the drug formulary is built into whichever carrier plan you choose, so you can't add GLP-1 coverage a la carte, you can only shop for a plan whose formulary already includes it. If your plan is self-funded or level-funded, you may have the option to design in a specific GLP-1 or weight-management rider. A broker can tell you which situation applies to your group and what either route would cost.

What if my plan denies coverage for a GLP-1 prescription?

Start with your doctor's office, they can often submit a formal appeal or additional documentation showing medical necessity. It's also worth asking about manufacturer savings cards, which can lower out-of-pocket cost for eligible patients even without insurance coverage, and confirming with your carrier exactly what their prior authorization criteria require before resubmitting.

Is GLP-1 coverage the same across every health plan?

No. Coverage varies significantly by carrier, by whether the plan is an individual marketplace plan or an employer group plan, and by the specific formulary each plan uses. Two people with the same diagnosis can have very different coverage outcomes depending on which plan and carrier they are enrolled in.
