Level-Funded Health Plans: Pros, Cons, and Who They Are Designed For

By Jade Benefits | Fort Lauderdale, FL | Updated November 2026

The short answer: a level-funded plan lets a small business pay a fixed monthly amount like a regular premium, while the business (not the insurance company) is responsible for claims up to a stop-loss limit. If your team is relatively healthy, you may get money back. If claims run high, you could see a bigger renewal increase. It fits healthy, stable groups that want predictable costs and a chance at savings, and it fits poorly for groups that need the most predictable, most protected coverage.

If you have ever been quoted a group health plan and heard the words "level-funded," it probably came with a lower price than the traditional plan next to it. That is not an accident, and it is not a trick. It is a different way of paying for the same kind of coverage, with a different set of trade-offs. Here is how it works, how it compares to fully insured and self-funded plans, and who it is actually designed for.

How a level-funded plan works

With a level-funded plan, your business pays the same fixed amount every month. That one payment is split into three parts:

  • A claims fund: money set aside to pay your employees' medical and prescription claims during the year.
  • Administration: the cost of running the plan, such as claims processing, network access, and customer service.
  • Stop-loss insurance: a safety net that kicks in when claims get unusually large. It usually works two ways: an individual limit that covers a single very large claim, and an aggregate limit that covers you if total claims for the whole group exceed a set amount.

At the end of the plan year, the claims that were actually paid are compared to the money set aside. If your group used less than expected, many carriers return some of the difference as a refund or a credit toward next year. If your group used more, stop-loss coverage protects you once you pass the limits in your contract. Refund terms are not the same everywhere, so it is worth asking exactly how yours works.

Level-funded vs fully insured vs self-funded

The easiest way to understand level-funded is to see it next to the two plans on either side of it.

In short: fully insured is the most predictable and protected, but you rarely see any upside. Self-funded has the most upside and the most risk, and is usually built for larger employers. Level-funded is the middle path: a self-funded structure with a fixed payment and stop-loss, designed so smaller businesses can use it.

Good to know: because level-funded plans are generally treated as self-funded, they are not priced the same way as ACA-regulated small group plans. Carriers can look at your group's health history, which is why you may be asked health questions, and why a healthy group may see a lower price than a fully insured quote. Rules differ by carrier and state, so confirm details before you decide.

Pros of level-funded plans

  • Predictable monthly cost. You pay one fixed amount, so budgeting is as easy as with a regular premium.
  • Possible refund. If your team uses less care than expected, you may get money back instead of the insurer keeping it.
  • Often lower upfront price for healthy groups. Pricing is based on your own group's history, so a healthy team can be rewarded.
  • Protection from big claims. Stop-loss coverage limits your exposure to one very large claim or an unusually costly year.
  • More flexibility in plan design. Because the plan is not locked to a standard small group template, carriers can often offer more customized benefits.
  • Reporting on your own claims. Many level-funded plans give you data on how your group uses care, which can help you make better decisions at renewal.

Cons and risks

  • Your renewal depends on your own claims. A high-claims year can raise your price, sometimes sharply. Fully insured plans are less tied to your group's own history.
  • Health questions and underwriting. Carriers typically review your group's health information. A group with a few high-cost members may get a higher price or no offer at all.
  • The refund is not guaranteed. Terms vary, and some plans return only part of the unused money, or give it as a credit instead of cash.
  • You still carry some risk. Your business is responsible for claims up to the stop-loss limits. That is more risk than a fully insured plan.
  • Different rules apply. Level-funded plans are generally treated as self-funded and may not have to include every benefit or protection that ACA small group plans do. Ask what is and is not covered.
  • More to understand. Stop-loss limits, claims run-out periods, and refund terms are details worth reading carefully. A good broker should walk you through them.

Who level-funded plans are designed for

They tend to fit well when:

  • Your team is relatively healthy and fairly stable, without a few very high-cost claimants.
  • You want a fixed monthly cost, but would like a chance to benefit when your team uses less care.
  • You are comfortable with some year-to-year change at renewal in exchange for potential savings.
  • Your group is large enough for carriers to quote, often around 10 or more employees, though some carriers go smaller.

They tend to fit poorly when:

  • You have employees or dependents with ongoing high-cost conditions. A fully insured plan is usually more stable for those groups.
  • You need the most predictable renewals and the most regulatory protection.
  • You do not want to deal with any claims-related variability at all.

None of these are rules. The right answer depends on your team, your budget, and what the carriers will offer for your group. The best approach is to quote both: a fully insured plan and a level-funded plan, side by side, and compare the price, protection, and renewal risk.

Questions to ask before you choose

  • What are the individual and aggregate stop-loss limits, and what happens to my business if we hit them?
  • How is the refund calculated, when is it paid, and is it cash or a credit?
  • How was my group priced, and what health information was used?
  • How are renewals set, and how much could the price move after a high-claims year?
  • Which benefits are covered, and does the plan include everything my employees expect?
  • Which provider network is used, and are my employees' doctors in it?

This guide is general information, not legal or tax advice. Level-funded plan rules and availability vary by carrier and state, so confirm details with a licensed broker before you decide.

Level-funded plan FAQ

What is a level-funded health plan?

A level-funded plan is a hybrid group health plan. Your business pays one fixed monthly amount that covers expected claims, administration, and stop-loss insurance. If claims come in lower than expected, you may get a refund or credit at the end of the year, depending on the carrier. If claims run high, stop-loss coverage limits what your business owes.

Is a level-funded plan the same as self-funded?

They are closely related. A level-funded plan is a form of self-funding, but with a fixed monthly payment and built-in stop-loss, so it feels more like a regular premium. A traditional self-funded plan has no fixed payment: the employer pays claims as they happen and carries more cost swings.

Do level-funded plans cover pre-existing conditions?

Yes, covered claims are paid regardless of when a condition started. But because pricing is based on your group's health history, carriers usually ask health questions during underwriting, and a group with higher expected claims can be priced higher or declined.

Do I get money back if my employees do not use their insurance?

Possibly. Many level-funded plans return unused claim dollars as a refund or a credit toward the next year, but it is not guaranteed and the terms vary by carrier. Ask exactly how the refund is calculated and when it is paid before you choose a plan.

How many employees do I need for a level-funded plan?

Minimums vary by carrier and state. Many carriers offer level-funded plans to groups of roughly 10 or more, and some go smaller. A broker can tell you which carriers will quote your group size in your state.

What happens at renewal on a level-funded plan?

Your renewal rate is based on your group's claims experience, so a high-claims year can raise your price, and a low-claims year can lower it. A big claim above the stop-loss limit may also affect your renewal. Review your options every year, not just the renewal offer.
