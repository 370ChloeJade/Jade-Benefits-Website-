1099 Health Insurance: A Complete Guide for Independent Contractors

By Jade Benefits | Fort Lauderdale, FL | Updated 2026

Getting a 1099 instead of a W-2 means no employer is handling health insurance for you, but it does not mean your options are limited or expensive. This guide covers exactly how 1099 workers, freelancers, and independent contractors get covered, what the 1099 form actually has to do with your insurance, how per diem pay factors in, and how to find out what you qualify for.

What the 1099 form has to do with insurance

A 1099 form reports income a business paid to you as a contractor rather than an employee. No 1099 status means no employer sponsored health plan, no automatic enrollment, and no employer contribution toward premiums. That single distinction is why 1099 workers have to actively shop for and enroll in their own coverage instead of picking from options an HR department already lined up.

The upside: you are not locked into whatever plan your employer chose. You can shop the entire market, pick the network and deductible that fits you, and in many cases pay less than a comparable employer plan once subsidies are factored in.

Your coverage options

  • ACA marketplace plans. The most common route, with plans ranging from bronze to platinum and subsidy eligibility based on your household income.
  • Private individual plans. Purchased directly through a carrier, sometimes useful outside open enrollment or for specific network needs.
  • Association or group plans. Some trade groups and professional associations offer group rates to member contractors.
  • Spouse's employer plan. If a spouse has employer coverage, adding yourself as a dependent is often the lowest cost option available.
  • Short term or catastrophic plans. Lower cost, narrower coverage, generally a stopgap rather than a long term plan.

Quick point: Most 1099 workers land on an ACA marketplace plan because it is the only option with income based subsidies attached. Comparing a subsidized marketplace plan against every other route is usually the fastest way to find the real lowest cost.

Subsidies and 1099 income

1099 income counts toward your household income for ACA subsidy calculations, same as W-2 wages. Because 1099 income can swing month to month, a few things are worth knowing:

  • You estimate your annual income when you enroll, and can update it if your income changes significantly during the year.
  • A meaningful income drop can trigger a special enrollment period, letting you adjust coverage outside the normal window.
  • Business expenses you deduct on your tax return generally lower your net income for subsidy purposes, which can increase the subsidy you qualify for.
  • The self-employed health insurance deduction can further reduce your taxable income, a separate benefit from the subsidy itself.

Many 1099 workers assume they earn too much for help and skip checking, then find out they qualify for a meaningful credit once they actually run the numbers.

Per diem pay and coverage

Workers who receive per diem payments, common in trucking, construction, and travel based work, often ask how that affects their insurance options. Per diem is generally a reimbursement for expenses like meals and lodging rather than taxable wages, so it is typically not counted toward income for subsidy purposes the same way 1099 earnings are. That said, exactly how your pay is structured matters, and workers paid a mix of 1099 income and per diem should get a real quote run against their actual numbers rather than guessing.

Choosing the right plan

With dozens of marketplace plans available in most areas, the right choice usually comes down to a few questions: How often do you see a doctor? Do you have a specialist or medication you need covered? Would a lower premium or a lower deductible serve you better given how unpredictable your income is? A broker can walk through these tradeoffs with your actual numbers instead of leaving you to compare twenty plans on a government website alone.

1099 insurance FAQ

What does the 1099 form have to do with health insurance?

A 1099 form reports income paid to an independent contractor rather than an employee. Because 1099 workers are not employees, they do not get employer sponsored health insurance and must buy their own coverage, either through the ACA marketplace, a private plan, or a group plan if they qualify through a spouse or association.

Can 1099 workers get subsidies for health insurance?

Yes. 1099 income counts toward your household income for ACA marketplace subsidy calculations. Many independent contractors qualify for premium tax credits that significantly lower monthly costs, especially since 1099 income can fluctuate and often qualifies for a special enrollment period when it changes.

Does per diem pay affect my health insurance options?

Per diem pay is generally not counted as taxable income for subsidy calculations since it reimburses actual expenses, but total reported income still determines your marketplace subsidy eligibility. Workers paid partly on a 1099 and partly per diem should review their exact numbers with a broker to get an accurate subsidy estimate.

Is it cheaper to get health insurance as a 1099 worker or through an employer?

Employer plans often look cheaper on paper because the employer pays part of the premium, but many 1099 workers qualify for ACA subsidies that bring marketplace plans down to a comparable or lower cost, especially at moderate income levels. Comparing both options with a broker is the only way to know for sure.
