Group Plan Renewing January 1? Here's Your Shopping Timeline

By Jade Benefits | Fort Lauderdale, FL | Updated September 2026

If your small business group health plan runs on a calendar year, your renewal date is January 1, which means the real decision-making window is happening right now, not in December. Carriers need time to underwrite, quote, and process paperwork, and the businesses that wait until the last few weeks end up with fewer options and less leverage. Here is the timeline we walk clients through so nothing gets rushed and nothing gets missed.

Why the timeline matters

Most small businesses receive a renewal notice from their current carrier roughly 60 to 90 days before their plan year ends, often with a premium increase already built in. If that notice is the first time you look at your options, you are negotiating from a position of no leverage: there is not enough time left to get competing quotes, compare networks, or run a proper open enrollment for your team. Starting earlier flips that. You get real comparisons across carriers, time to fix any issues that turn up, and room to communicate changes to your employees instead of surprising them in the last week of December.

Your renewal shopping timeline

For a January 1 renewal, here is roughly how the months break down:

  • 90 days out (early October) Pull your current plan details and claims summary, and get a rough headcount and budget in mind. This is when a broker can start a real market comparison rather than a rush job.
  • 60 days out (early November) Review quotes from multiple carriers side by side: premiums, networks, deductibles, and prescription coverage, not just the bottom line number. Flag any changes to your team's doctors or medications now, while there is time to check them against a new network.
  • 30 to 45 days out (late November to early December) Make your final carrier and plan decision, and set your employee contribution strategy if it is changing. This gives enough runway to prepare enrollment materials before the holidays slow everything down.
  • 2 to 3 weeks out (mid-December) Run employee enrollment: collect elections, dependent information, and any required forms. A broker can host a short meeting so your team understands what changed and why.
  • January 1 New or renewed coverage takes effect. If you started early enough, this is a non-event: cards arrive, coverage continues, and nobody scrambles.

Rule of thumb: if today's date is more than 90 days before your renewal, you have full flexibility. Inside 30 days, your options shrink fast, since most carriers need lead time to underwrite a new group.

What's different heading into 2027

A few things make this renewal season worth a closer look rather than an automatic rollover of last year's plan. The enhanced premium tax credits that lowered individual marketplace premiums since 2021 have expired, which means some employees and their family members who previously bought their own marketplace plan may be more interested in employer group coverage this year, since group plans are not affected by that subsidy change the same way. See our breakdown of why individual marketplace premiums went up in 2026 for the full picture, since it affects how attractive your group offering looks to employees by comparison. On top of that, individual marketplace open enrollment for 2027 coverage is running November 1 through January 15, overlapping directly with the tail end of most group renewal timelines, so employees may be fielding messaging from both sides at once. Being clear and early with your own team helps you control that conversation instead of leaving it to guesswork.

Questions to ask before you renew

  • Did our claims experience go up or down this year, and does that explain the renewal increase we were quoted?
  • Are our employees' current doctors and prescriptions still in network on the renewal plan?
  • Is our contribution strategy still competitive for hiring and retention, or has the market shifted?
  • Would a different plan type, or a different carrier at a similar price point, offer better coverage for the same budget?
  • If we switch carriers, can the new plan start January 1 with no coverage gap?

Common mistakes to avoid

The most common renewal mistakes are all versions of the same problem: waiting too long. Auto-renewing without comparing other carriers means accepting whatever increase shows up on the notice, even when a better option exists. Waiting until December to start the process leaves no time to properly vet a new carrier, so businesses default back to their current plan even when it is not the best fit. And skipping the employee communication step until the last minute creates confusion right when your team needs clarity most. None of these take more than a few extra weeks to avoid if you start on the timeline above.

Frequently asked questions

When do most small group health plans renew?

Many small businesses set up their group plan with a January 1 effective date, so their annual renewal also falls on January 1. That said, your renewal date is whatever date you originally enrolled, so check your plan documents or ask your broker if you are not sure.

How far in advance should I start shopping for renewal?

Start 60 to 90 days before your renewal date. For a January 1 renewal, that means beginning in September or October. Waiting until 30 days out significantly narrows your options, since carriers need time to underwrite and quote.

Will my group health premium go up in 2027?

Most small groups see some increase at renewal based on claims history and broader market trends, but the size varies by carrier, plan, and your group's own claims. Shopping multiple carriers before you renew, rather than accepting the auto-renewal quote, is the best way to find out whether a comparable or better plan is available for less.

Can I change group health carriers at renewal without a gap in coverage?

Yes. If you start the process early enough, a broker can time a new carrier's effective date to begin the day after your current plan ends, so your employees see no gap in coverage.
