Why roofing and construction crews are hard to insure well

Contractor crews are a tough fit for off the shelf insurance shopping. Headcounts move up and down with the season, jobs and job sites change constantly, and most owners are juggling a mix of long term W-2 employees and 1099 subcontractors on the same project. National insurance sites are not built to sort through that, so a lot of roofing and construction owners either overpay for a generic plan or give up on offering coverage at all. A broker who understands how crews are actually staffed can build a plan around your real payroll instead of a template.

Health insurance vs. workers' compensation

This is the single most common point of confusion we run into with contractors, so it is worth being direct about it: workers' compensation and group health insurance are not the same coverage, and one does not replace the other.

  • Workers' compensation covers injuries and illnesses that happen because of the job, and is typically required by state law once you hit a certain employee count.
  • Group health insurance covers your crew and their families for everyday medical care, that is not job related, doctor visits, prescriptions, hospital stays, and preventive care.

A crew member who breaks an arm on a roof needs workers' comp. That same crew member's kid with strep throat, or their own annual physical, needs health insurance. Most contractors ultimately need both, and having a real health plan on top of required workers' comp is exactly what separates a contractor who is easy to work for from one that is not.

Structuring coverage for mixed W-2 and 1099 crews

Most roofing and construction businesses run a blend of payroll employees and subcontracted labor, and that mix affects how a group plan gets built:

  • W-2 crew and office staff are typically who a group health plan is built around. Eligibility usually depends on hours worked and how long they have been with you.
  • 1099 subcontractors are independent businesses, not employees, so they are generally not eligible for your company's group plan. We can still help individual subcontractors shop their own coverage separately, at no cost to either of you.
  • Seasonal and per-project hires can often be handled with a waiting period or eligibility threshold, so your core year-round crew gets coverage without you having to insure every short term hire.

Bottom line: you do not have to cover every person on a job site to offer real benefits. A plan built around your core W-2 crew, with individual options available for subcontractors, covers the people you most want to retain without overcomplicating payroll.

What it costs a small contractor

Costs vary based on your state, the carrier, your crew's ages, and how the plan is structured, so there is no single number that applies to every contractor. Small group plans are generally available to businesses with as few as 2 enrolled employees, and many owners choose to contribute a fixed percentage or dollar amount per employee rather than covering the full premium, which keeps the cost predictable while still giving your crew real coverage. The only way to know your actual number is to get a quote run against your specific headcount.

Contractor health insurance FAQ

Can a small roofing or construction company get group health insurance?

Yes. Small group health plans are available to businesses with as few as 2 enrolled employees. Crew size, not company size, is usually what carriers look at, so a small roofing outfit or construction crew can qualify the same as a larger contractor.

Does health insurance cover job site injuries instead of workers' compensation?

No. Workers' compensation and group health insurance are separate and both matter. Workers' comp covers job related injuries and is typically required by state law. Group health insurance covers everyday medical care, illness, prescriptions, and non job related injuries for your crew and their families. Most contractors need both.

Can I offer health insurance to W-2 crew but not 1099 subcontractors?

Yes. Group health plans are generally built around W-2 payroll employees. 1099 subcontractors are independent businesses and typically shop their own individual coverage, which we can also help them compare, but they are usually not eligible for your company group plan.

How much does health insurance cost for a roofing or construction crew?

It varies by state, carrier, crew age, and plan design, but small group plans commonly run a few hundred dollars per employee per month before any employer contribution strategy is applied. A broker can run exact numbers for your specific crew at no cost.
