Small Business Health Insurance Cost in 2026: What You'll Actually Pay

By Jade Benefits | Fort Lauderdale, FL | Updated 2026

"How much is this going to cost me?" is the first question every small business owner asks before offering health insurance, and it is a fair one. The answer depends on more than a single sticker price. This guide breaks down what small business health insurance actually costs in 2026, the factors that push your number up or down, the tax credits that can offset the bill, and how to get pricing built around your specific team instead of a generic estimate.

Average cost per employee

Most small businesses land somewhere between 300 and 600 dollars per employee per month for group health coverage before any employer contribution is applied. That range is wide because it covers everything from a lean high deductible plan to a rich, low deductible PPO. A few general benchmarks to plan around:

Coverage typeTypical monthly range
Employee only300 to 500 dollars
Employee plus spouse650 to 950 dollars
Employee plus family950 to 1,500 dollars

These figures are the full premium, not what the employee pays out of pocket. Once you decide your contribution level, the employee only sees their share of the bill, which is often a fraction of the total.

What drives your price up or down

No two small business quotes look the same because pricing is built from several moving parts:

  • Group size and makeup. The age and location of your team affects the base rate carriers offer.
  • Plan type. HMO plans are typically the most affordable, PPO plans cost more but offer wider provider access, and EPO plans usually land in between.
  • Deductible level. A higher deductible plan lowers your monthly premium in exchange for higher out of pocket costs when care is used.
  • Network and location. Rates vary by state and even by county based on local hospital and provider pricing.
  • Employer contribution. This does not change the total premium, but it changes what you pay versus what your employees pay.
  • Ancillary add ons. Dental, vision, and life insurance are usually priced separately and add a modest amount per employee.

Quick point: The sticker price on a single plan means very little on its own. What matters is how the total cost compares across several A rated carriers for your exact group, which is the entire point of shopping with a broker instead of a single insurer.

How to lower your premium

You have more control over the final number than most owners realize:

  • Set a partial contribution. Covering 50 to 75 percent of the employee only premium is common and keeps your budget predictable.
  • Consider a higher deductible plan paired with an HSA. Lower monthly premiums, and employees build tax advantaged savings for care.
  • Right size the network. A narrower, high performing network can cost meaningfully less than a broad national PPO for teams that mostly see local providers.
  • Bundle ancillary benefits. Adding dental and vision through the same carrier often comes at a lower combined rate than buying separately.
  • Shop every renewal. Carrier pricing shifts year to year, and a renewal review can catch savings you would otherwise miss.

Tax credits and deductions

Cost conversations often stop at the premium, but the effective cost is usually lower once you factor in tax treatment:

  • Small Business Health Care Tax Credit. Businesses with fewer than 25 full time equivalent employees and average annual wages under a set threshold may qualify for a credit worth up to 50 percent of premiums paid.
  • Deductible contributions. Premiums your business pays toward employee coverage are generally deductible as an ordinary business expense.
  • Section 125 cafeteria plans. Letting employees pay their share of premiums with pretax dollars lowers payroll taxes for both the business and the employee.

Eligibility rules change and depend on your specific numbers, so it is worth confirming your situation with a tax professional alongside your broker.

Cost FAQ

How much does small business health insurance cost per employee?

Most small businesses pay somewhere between 300 and 600 dollars per employee per month before any employer contribution, though the exact number depends on your location, team age, plan design, and how much of the premium you choose to cover. Family coverage runs higher than employee only coverage.

Do small businesses have to pay the full premium?

No. You decide how much of the premium your business covers. Many small employers start by covering a meaningful share, such as 50 to 100 percent of the employee only premium, and let employees pay the difference for dependents.

Are there tax credits for small business health insurance?

Businesses with fewer than 25 full time equivalent employees and average wages under a certain threshold may qualify for the Small Business Health Care Tax Credit, worth up to 50 percent of premiums paid. Employer premium contributions are also generally tax deductible as a business expense.

Is it cheaper to use a broker for small business health insurance?

Using a licensed broker does not cost you anything extra. Brokers are paid by the insurance carrier, not the employer, so you get side by side quotes and ongoing support at no added cost while often finding better pricing than shopping alone.
