Health Insurance for Real Estate Agencies & Agents

Group plans for brokerage staff | Individual guidance for 1099 agents | Free to you

Real estate brokerages sit in an unusual spot when it comes to health insurance. Your office staff are W-2 employees who can join a standard group health plan, but most of your agents are independent contractors who cannot. Understanding that split, and what you can realistically offer each group, is the first step to using benefits as a real recruiting and retention tool instead of an afterthought.

Staff coverage vs. agent coverage

Most brokerages have two very different groups of people working under the same roof: W-2 office staff, like transaction coordinators, marketing, and admin, and 1099 independent contractor agents. Insurance rules treat them differently, and knowing the difference up front avoids a lot of confusion when you start shopping for coverage.

Group coverage for your office staff

Your W-2 employees can typically be enrolled in a standard small group health plan, the same as any other small business. Group plans are generally available starting around 2 enrolled employees, which covers most independent brokerages, not just larger firms. This is often the easiest place to start if you want to offer real benefits without navigating the complexity of covering independent agents.

Helping your agents with individual coverage

Because most agents are classified as 1099 independent contractors, they are generally not eligible for your brokerage's group plan. That does not mean there is nothing you can offer them. Agents can shop individual and ACA marketplace plans on their own, and many qualify for premium subsidies that lower the cost significantly. A brokerage that proactively connects its agents with a broker who can walk them through those options, at no cost to the agent or the brokerage, is offering real support that a lot of competing offices do not think to provide.

Recruiting angle: "We'll help you sort out your health insurance" is a sentence that matters to an agent weighing offers between brokerages, even when the coverage itself is individual, not group.

What it costs a small brokerage

Cost for staff group coverage depends on your state, the carrier, team size, and plan design, so there is no single number that fits every office. Many brokerages contribute a fixed amount per employee rather than the full premium to keep costs predictable. Helping agents shop individual coverage is free, since brokers are paid by the carrier, not by the agent or the agency.

Real estate health insurance FAQ

Can a real estate brokerage offer group health insurance to office staff?

Yes. Group health plans are built around W-2 payroll employees, so brokerage staff like transaction coordinators, admins, and marketing staff can typically be enrolled in a group plan starting around 2 eligible employees.

Are real estate agents usually eligible for a brokerage's group health plan?

Most real estate agents are classified as independent contractors, not W-2 employees, which generally makes them ineligible for a brokerage's group plan. Agents typically need to shop individual health coverage on their own, which a broker can also help with at no cost.

Can offering benefits help a brokerage recruit more agents?

It can help indirectly. While agents themselves usually cannot join a brokerage's group plan, offering to guide agents through individual coverage options, subsidy eligibility, and open enrollment is a meaningful support benefit that differentiates a brokerage in a recruiting conversation.

What does group health insurance cost for a small real estate office?

It depends on your state, carrier, staff size, and plan design. Many brokerages contribute a fixed amount per employee rather than the full premium to keep costs predictable. A broker can run exact numbers for your office at no cost.
